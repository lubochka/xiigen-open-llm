### 19.6.7 What has been closed

This subsection closes the INCRT issue in a stronger sense than simple compatibility. A-MHB-G gives INCRT an explicit data structure. Heads are anchored dictionary atoms; their loads, entropies, routing contributions, and spectral effects are measurable. Growth adds a new bilinear interaction mode. Pruning removes unused modes. Freezing protects mature modes. DDCL supplies the conceptual interpretation of anchors as prototypes, but the chapter does not need the full DDCL machinery.

Consequently, the remaining research question is not whether A-MHB-G can be made incremental. It can. The remaining question is empirical: which growth signal, residual novelty rule, and pruning tolerance give the best compute-performance trade-off in realistic training regimes? That question belongs to the experimental programme after Volume I, whereas the architectural obstruction itself is resolved here.

<a id="section-19-7"></a>
### 19.7 Variants that close residual design questions

The remaining design questions concern causality, long context, the relation with MoE, and interpretability. None requires changing the bilinear synthesis primitive; each is obtained by changing the routing or the object through which the operator is read.

<a id="section-19-7-1"></a>
#### 19.7.1 Causal A-MHB-G

The bidirectional operator of Section 19.3.2 is the natural setting for Volume I. The autoregressive counterpart is obtained by replacing the unrestricted partner set with a causal partner set

$$
T_k^{-}(i)=\operatorname{TopK}\{S_{ij}:1\le j\le i\}.
$$

Equivalently, one sets $S_{ij}=-\infty$ for $j>i$ before applying top-$k$. The causal layer is

<a id="eq-19-5"></a>
<a id="equation-19-5"></a>
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A\text{-MHBG}(x_i,X)&=\sigma\bigl(\widetilde B_i^{-}(X)W_O\bigr),\\
\widetilde B_i^{-}(X)&=\sum_{j\in T_k^{-}(i)}\alpha_{ij}
\sum_{h=1}^{H}g_h(x_i)(x_iW_L^h)\odot(x_jW_R^h).
\end{aligned}
\tag{19.5}
$$

This closes the architectural obstruction to decoder-only models. The empirical performance of the causal variant belongs naturally to Volume II, but the definition is immediate and preserves the routed bilinear primitive.

<a id="section-19-7-2"></a>
#### 19.7.2 Dictionary-routed A-MHB-G for long context

Dense A-MHB-G forms the full score matrix $XW_sX^\top$, and therefore retains a quadratic partner-search cost in sequence length. This cost is not intrinsic to the bilinear operator; it belongs to the dense search implementation. A long-context variant introduces a dictionary

$$
C=\{c_1,\ldots,c_M\}\subset\mathbb R^d,\qquad M\ll n.
$$

Each token first selects a small set of dictionary cells,

$$
Q(i)=\operatorname{TopK}\{x_iW_cc_m^\top:m=1,\ldots,M\},
$$

and then searches only among tokens assigned to those cells. The partner set becomes

$$
T_k^{\mathrm{dict}}(i)=\operatorname{TopK}\{S_{ij}:j\in\mathcal B(Q(i))\},
$$

<a id="pdf-0782"></a>
## PDF 782 · Book page 746

where $\mathcal B(Q(i))$ is the union of the selected buckets. If bucket sizes are controlled, the dense $O(n^2d)$ search is replaced by a dictionary-routed cost of the form

$$
O(nMd+nkd),\qquad M\ll n.
$$

This does not prove a complete long-context implementation, but it resolves the conceptual issue: sub-quadratic A-MHB-G is obtained by changing partner search, not by changing the bilinear synthesis operator.

<a id="section-19-7-3"></a>
#### 19.7.3 A-MHB-G as self-generated MoE

Standard MoE layers route tokens to parameterised experts:

$$
y_i=\sum_{e\in E_k(i)}p_{ie}f_e(x_i).
$$

A-MHB-G instead routes a token to partner tokens:

$$
y_i=\sigma\left(\sum_{j\in T_k(i)}\alpha_{ij}F(x_i,x_j)W_O\right),
$$

where

$$
F(x_i,x_j)=\sum_hg_h(x_i)(x_iW_L^h)\odot(x_jW_R^h).
$$

Thus the selected partners act as dynamic, data-dependent experts generated by the sequence itself. This gives a precise conceptual relation to MoE: A-MHB-G is a self-generated MoE in which the expert pool is contextual rather than a fixed collection of subnetworks.

<a id="section-19-7-4"></a>
#### 19.7.4 Interpretability as a bilinear interaction graph

A-MHB-G does not produce ordinary attention maps. Its interpretability object is a sparse directed graph

$$
G_X=(V,E_X),\qquad(i,j)\in E_X\Longleftrightarrow j\in T_k(i),
$$

with edge weights $\alpha_{ij}$ and edge features

$$
\phi_{ij}=\sum_hg_h(x_i)(x_iW_L^h)\odot(x_jW_R^h).
$$

Edges identify which partners are used; weights quantify routing strength; features describe which bilinear channels are activated; anchors identify which head geometry participates. This is richer than an attention matrix because it separates partner selection, head allocation, and feature synthesis.

<a id="section-19-8"></a>
### 19.8 Related work and position vs SOTA

We position MHB-G against the recent landscape of efficient Transformer architectures along the four design axes that characterise it:

(1) Bilinear cross-token interactions ($\odot$ on token pairs);

(2) Top-$k$ content-dependent token-to-token routing;

(3) Multi-head $\sum_h$ structure;

<a id="pdf-0783"></a>
## PDF 783 · Book page 747

(4) Drop-in replacement of MHA + MLP (both, simultaneously).

The literature search identified seven families of related work. To the best of our knowledge, none combines the four axes simultaneously in the same primitive. The closest works either unify attention and FFN through shared experts, introduce multiplicative gates inside the MLP, or sparsify attention while preserving the attention/value-aggregation paradigm. A-MHB-G differs by using selected partner tokens themselves as the objects of bilinear feature synthesis.

<a id="section-19-8-1"></a>
#### 19.8.1 Family 1 — Bilinear / Multimodal Pooling

Kim et al. (ICLR 2017), BAN (NeurIPS 2018), Tenenbaum & Freeman (Neural Computation 2000). Use Hadamard product for bilinear pooling but cross modality (image × text), not cross-token within a sequence.

<a id="section-19-8-2"></a>
#### 19.8.2 Family 2 — gMLP / SGU / MLP-Mixer

Liu et al. (NeurIPS 2021), Tolstikhin et al. (NeurIPS 2021), Touvron et al. (T-PAMI 2022). Spatial Gating Unit uses $u\odot Vz$ with static spatial $V$ — not content-dependent. Reported failure on sentence-pair tasks (MNLI), suggesting that data-dependent cross-token interaction is necessary.

<a id="section-19-8-3"></a>
#### 19.8.3 Family 3 — Multi-Head FFN

MHFFN, FlashMHF. Multi-head structure on FFN, but no cross-token bilinear, no routing.

<a id="section-19-8-4"></a>
#### 19.8.4 Family 4 — MoE / Expert-Choice

Switch Transformer (JMLR 2022), Mixtral (2024), Expert-Choice (NeurIPS 2022), Multi-Head MoE (Wu et al. 2024). Top-$k$ routing token $\to$ expert FFN (not token $\to$ token). Replaces MLP only; MHA remains.

<a id="section-19-8-5"></a>
#### 19.8.5 Family 5 — Sparse / Dynamic Attention

Longformer (Beltagy et al. 2020), Routing Transformers (Roy et al. 2021), DTRNet (arxiv 2509.00925), Token Sparse Attention (arxiv 2602.03216). Optimisations of attention sparsity, not new operators replacing MHA + MLP.

<a id="section-19-8-6"></a>
#### 19.8.6 Family 6 — Multiplicative Interactions

GLU/GeGLU/SwiGLU (Shazeer 2020), FLASH (Hua et al. 2022), Jayakumar et al. (ICLR 2020). Hadamard-based gating in MLP, but within a single token (no cross-token interaction).

<a id="section-19-8-7"></a>
#### 19.8.7 Family 7 — UMoE: Unifying Attention and FFN

Yang et al. (NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2505.07260) is conceptually the closest family because it aims at unifying attention and FFN computation. The unification mechanism, however, is different: UMoE relies on shared expert subnetworks, whereas A-MHB-G replaces the expert pool by bilinear interactions between selected token partners. In UMoE the primitive remains expert-centric; in A-MHB-G it is token-pair-centric. This difference is not cosmetic: it changes the interpretability object from expert assignment to a sparse bilinear interaction graph.

### 19.9 Empirical illustration

<a id="section-19-9-1"></a>
#### 19.9.1 Experimental setup

We compare four block architectures within an otherwise identical BERT-like model ($d=768$, $H=12$, 6 layers reduced from 12 for budget, $n=128$). These runs were launched for the base MHB-G operator before the anchored refinement was introduced. They should therefore be read as an empirical validation of the bilinear routed core. Since the anchor branch adds only $Hd$ parameters and does not change the token-pair primitive, the results support the core of A-MHB-G but do not yet constitute a full benchmark of the anchored variant:

(1) **Standard:** MHA + MLP with residual and LN (BERT-like baseline);

(2) **SwiGLU:** MHA + SwiGLU (Shazeer 2020), $2/3$ width adjustment;

(3) **MHB-G:** this chapter, with residual;

(4) **MHB-G no-residual:** this chapter, without residual — the test of Lemma 19.6.

**Pretraining.** Masked Language Modelling on WikiText-2, 10 000 steps, AdamW with linear warmup + decay (1 000 warmup steps), batch size 32, peak learning rate $5\times10^{-4}$, weight decay 0.01.

**Fine-tuning.** Three GLUE tasks (SST-2 single-sentence, MRPC sentence-pair, RTE sentence-pair), 3 epochs each, AdamW with $2\times10^{-5}$ learning rate.

**Hardware.** Kaggle P100 (16 GB), $\sim1.5$ hours total runtime.

**Caveat on regime.** The configuration above is a proof-of-concept setup, not a benchmark-grade pretraining: 10 000 steps on WikiText-2 are roughly two orders of magnitude shorter than typical BERT pretraining. We therefore expect all four models to be undertrained, which has implications for the interpretation of the results below.
