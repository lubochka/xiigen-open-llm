## GOOD / BAD / EDGE and why

GOOD: one shared dictionary gives each root a stable meaning in both stored examples and future queries; unrelated requests do not renumber its coordinates. GOOD: before/after is encoded as an event relation and consumed by the scorer; `?.` and `??` retain separate operator roles; same-referent agreement survives local ID renaming.

BAD: independently fit two dictionaries, pad both to 60 and compare their positions as shared semantics. BAD: preserve exact stored pair text but erase its only temporal or negation distinction before learning. BAD: flatten words into hash buckets and claim dependency reasoning without a relation reader. BAD: generalize one Latin-only regex path into a claim that all encoders reject Russian.

EDGE: unseen root; vocabulary order change; more than 32 roots; Unicode NFC/NFD or ё/е; ambiguous relation; unsupported parser; zero vector; collision; new dialect; same-width old checkpoint. Return explicit UNKNOWN, a compatible parent or a scoped migration, not invented certainty. For an incremental BPE consumer distinguish byte pieces from whole words and delegate its prefix-state detail to 02D; do not turn this into an unrelated decoder rewrite.
